THE ROAD — every roll, drop and checkpoint
Strict top-down: the first rule whose condition is true fires. Only actions and conditions you have been given appear.
Aether is experience. It levels units, and each unit grows on its own curve — Dorrek gains 48 HP and 2.4 DEF a level where Vey gains 21 HP and 3.2 SPD. Growth is predetermined, not allocated: character identity rather than player choice.

The old stat-node grid is retired. Six very different allocations of the same node budget produced a depth spread of 1.04× (1.24× with the clock) — allocation was expressive but not consequential, against an action and gambit axis worth up to +173%. Trading it for growth curves costs no measurable build diversity and buys distinct characters. Solo still needs ~4% compounding growth per wave; the EXP curve is calibrated to put a solo character at LV 14 ≈ 2.05× by wave 20.
Lore upgrades actions, not characters — that is what keeps it distinct from Aether. It comes from duplicate drops, so it stays at zero until drops turn random after the first boss. No bonus is universally best: Lasting is the strongest pick on an action that applies a status and worth literally nothing on one that doesn't.
Marks are the gacha currency. One pull, one cost, rolling across companions, actions and gambit conditions. Anything you already hold converts instead of being wasted.
Curated to wave 20 — an action every 2 waves, a condition between, each landing just before the threat it answers. Random thereafter.

THE CLOCK (v1.0, on by default)
After 8 of its own turns, an enemy gains +5% ATK per turn it takes, compounding. Counted in the enemy's own turns because CTB has no rounds and beats are abstract — a stack that ticks when you watch that unit act is the only legible unit. A Slowed enemy therefore enrages more slowly, so Cripple answers the clock as well as the damage.

This is what makes healing optional. Without a clock, a heal rule is unbounded sustain and nothing else is, so it is worth +173% and every build converges on it. With the clock, out-sustaining stops working: solo, heal drops to −2.3% against the best non-heal build, and in a pair to −10.5%, where Cripple becomes the best answer. Turn it off to feel the difference — with it off, a heal build runs to the wave cap and nothing else gets close.
